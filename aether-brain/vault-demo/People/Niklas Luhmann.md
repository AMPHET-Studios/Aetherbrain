# Niklas Luhmann

German sociologist (1927–1998), best known in academia for his theory of social systems — and, among note-takers, for his slip-box.

## Life in brief
- Trained in law and worked as a civil servant before turning to sociology
- Spent a year at Harvard in the early 1960s, where he studied with Talcott Parsons
- Professor of sociology at Bielefeld University from 1968 until his retirement
- Published around 70 books and several hundred articles

## The slip-box
Luhmann kept a [[Zettelkasten]] of roughly 90,000 cards. Its power came from three features:
- **Fixed addresses** that let new cards branch off old ones, so a line of thought could grow in the middle
- **Cross-references** between distant cards
- **A keyword register** with only a few entry points per term — an early cousin of [[Maps of Content]]

He wrote about the system in a 1981 essay that treats the box as a communication partner — my paraphrase lives in [[Quote - Luhmann on surprise]].

## Why he matters to me
He shows that a well-linked collection of small notes can end up knowing more than the person who wrote them. That's the bet behind [[Evergreen Notes]] and behind [[Aether Brain]].
