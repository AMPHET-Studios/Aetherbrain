# Composition

The arrangement that decides where the eye goes, in what order, and where it rests.

## Tools
- **Focal point** — one clear winner, supported by the [[Value Structure#Three-Value Rule]]
- **Big, medium, small** — vary shape sizes; equal sizes feel static
- **Leading lines** — roads, roots and light shafts that point inward
- **Framing** — foreground elements that contain the view
- **Tangents** — avoid edges that just kiss; overlap them or separate them clearly

## Rule of thirds
A fine starting crutch. The better question: where is the tension, and where is the rest?

## Environment concept art
- Foreground, midground and background, each with its own value range
- Scale cues: a tiny figure makes a ruin monumental
- The path of the eye should match the path of the player — see [[Environmental Storytelling]]
- Perspective choice is a composition choice: a low horizon for awe, a high one for overview — [[Perspective Fundamentals]]

## Process
I solve composition at [[Thumbnailing|thumbnail]] size, arranging big blocks of [[Shape Language]] before any detail exists. If the thumbnail doesn't work, the painting won't either — rendering only amplifies what's already there.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Can I point to the focal point in under a second?
- [ ] Is there a place for the eye to rest?
- [ ] Does anything lead the eye out of the frame?
