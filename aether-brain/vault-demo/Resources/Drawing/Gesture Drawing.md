# Gesture Drawing

Short timed drawings — 30 seconds to 2 minutes — that capture the action and energy of a pose before any anatomy or detail. The classic reference is Kimon Nicolaides' *The Natural Way to Draw*: draw what the figure is *doing*, not what it looks like.

## Line of Action
One long curve through the whole figure, usually from the head down through the weight-bearing leg. C-curves and S-curves read as alive; straight lines read as stiff. Push it further than the reference — exaggeration survives the rendering stage, timidity doesn't.

## Session structure
| Duration | Count | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 10 | line of action only |
| 1 min | 10 | add the tilt of ribcage and pelvis |
| 2 min | 5 | big shapes and weight |

## What it trains
- Seeing the big idea first — the same muscle as [[Thumbnailing]]
- Readable poses, which feed straight into [[Silhouette Design]]
- Rhythm between straights and curves, the root of [[Shape Language]]

## Beyond figures
Gesture works on anything: trees, creatures, vehicles, even a monolith leaning into the ground. Quick gestural boxes are also the best warm-up for [[Perspective Fundamentals]].

## Where it lives
A daily standard in [[Creative Practice]], done the [[Deliberate Practice]] way — one focus per session, compare against the reference at the end.
