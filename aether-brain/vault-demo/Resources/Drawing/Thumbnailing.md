# Thumbnailing

Small, fast, rough drawings for exploring many ideas before committing to one. For me: about 2–3 inches wide, 2–5 minutes each, three or four values, no detail. #concept-art

## Why small
- You can't render at this size, so you're forced to think in big shapes and [[Value Structure]]
- Speed means volume; volume buries the first idea, which is usually the obvious one
- A bad thumbnail costs two minutes, not two days

## Process
1. Write the brief in one sentence
2. Fill a page with 12–20 frames at a consistent aspect ratio
3. Vary one thing deliberately: camera height, focal point, time of day
4. Pick three by criteria, not mood: does it read, is the focal point clear, does it fit the brief?
5. Develop one at a larger size

## Checks
- [[Composition]]: tangents, eye path, rest areas
- [[Silhouette Design]]: do the key shapes read in black?

## As practice
Thumbnails make great [[Deliberate Practice]]: one variable per page, compare at the end. They're also step one of every [[Concept Art Portfolio 2026]] piece.

## A note-taking parallel
A thumbnail is to a painting what an atomic note is to an essay — see [[Zettelkasten#Atomicity]]. Small units, cheap to make, easy to recombine.
