# Perspective Fundamentals

Perspective is how I convince a flat surface that it's a space. The rules are simple; the hard part is drawing them freehand with confidence.

## The essentials
- **Horizon line = eye level.** Everything above it we see from below; everything below it, from above
- **Vanishing points** sit on the horizon for horizontal parallel lines; one-, two- and three-point perspective just count how many sets of edges converge
- **Ellipses:** the minor axis lines up with the axis of the cylinder, and ellipses open up (get rounder) as they move away from the horizon
- **Scale by horizon:** when the viewer stands on the same ground as the figures, heads line up roughly on the horizon

## Why it matters for 3D
Drawing a box in space is the same mental act as a blockout in Blender. Confident perspective sketches make [[Hard-Surface Modeling]] faster, and placing a camera in a level is [[Composition]] with a real lens.
