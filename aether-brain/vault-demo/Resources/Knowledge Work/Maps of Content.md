# Maps of Content

A map of content (MOC) is a note whose job is to gather links on a topic — a hub that emerges once a cluster grows too big to hold in my head. The term comes from Nick Milo's Linking Your Thinking approach.

## When to make one
Not up front. Make a map when you hit a "mental squeeze point": you keep searching for the same five notes, or a topic has a dozen notes and no entry point.

## Maps vs. folders
A folder forces each note into exactly one place — [[PARA Method|PARA]] uses folders only for actionability. A note can live in many maps at once. That's [[Zettelkasten#Links over folders]] applied one level up.

## A good map
- Has a sentence of context per link, not just a list
- Groups links into a few sections that reflect how I think about the topic
- Includes open questions, so it's a workbench as well as a table of contents

## In 3D
In a [[Force-Directed Layout]], maps become high-degree hubs that pull their cluster together — natural landmarks when flying through the graph.
