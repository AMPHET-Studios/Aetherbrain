# Deliberate Practice

Anders Ericsson's term for the kind of practice that actually builds expertise, as opposed to simply logging hours. His book *Peak* (with Robert Pool) pushes back on the idea that time alone makes experts; *how* you practice matters more than how long.

## Ingredients
- A specific goal just beyond current ability
- Full attention — tired practice mostly rehearses mistakes
- Immediate, honest feedback
- Building better mental representations: a sharper internal model of what "good" looks like

## Applied to drawing
- [[Gesture Drawing]]: 30-second poses, then compare against the photo for the [[Gesture Drawing#Line of Action|line of action]]
- [[Perspective Fundamentals]]: draw 50 ellipses, then check the axes with a ruler
- Master copies limited to three values — [[Value Structure#Three-Value Rule]]

## Supporting habits
Deliberate practice is draining, so it needs scaffolding: a fixed time, a tiny starting ritual, a visible streak. The habit-design ideas in [[Atomic Habits]] help here — make the cue obvious and the first step small.
