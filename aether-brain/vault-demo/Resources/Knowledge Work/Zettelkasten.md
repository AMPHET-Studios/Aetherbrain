# Zettelkasten

A note-taking method made famous by [[Niklas Luhmann]], who kept roughly 90,000 index cards over more than four decades and credited them with much of his enormous output. The point isn't storage; it's building a thinking partner that grows through links.

## Atomicity
One idea per note, in my own words, understandable without the source open. Atomic notes can be recombined; chapter-sized notes can only be re-read. If a note needs a heading halfway down, it probably wants to be two notes. This is the same move as [[Evergreen Notes]] — and, oddly, the same move as sketching small before painting big.

## Links over folders
Luhmann gave each card a fixed address (like 21/3d7a) and branched new thoughts off existing ones, then cross-referenced freely. Structure emerges from links, not from choosing the right folder up front. Folders still help with *action* — that's what [[PARA Method|PARA]] is for — but meaning lives in the connections, and hubs like [[Maps of Content]] appear once a cluster gets dense.

## In practice
1. Capture fleeting notes anywhere
2. Once a day, turn the keepers into permanent notes
3. Link each new note to at least two existing ones, and write *why*
4. Compress older notes with [[Progressive Summarization]] when they come back into use

The [[Memex]] imagined trails; the slip-box made them practical by hand; a [[Force-Directed Layout]] finally lets me *see* them.
