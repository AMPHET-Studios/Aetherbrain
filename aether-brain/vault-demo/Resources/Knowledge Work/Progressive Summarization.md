# Progressive Summarization

Tiago Forte's technique for compressing notes in layers — but *only when you touch them again*.

1. **Layer 1** — the captured passage
2. **Layer 2** — bold the important parts
3. **Layer 3** — highlight the best of the bold
4. **Layer 4** — a short summary in my own words at the top
5. **Layer 5** — remix it into something new

## The key idea
Compression is opportunistic. You don't summarize everything up front; you add a layer each time a note proves useful. Notes that never come back never cost you effort, and notes that come back often become faster to use every time.

## A painter's version
It's the [[Value Structure#Squint Test]] for text. Squint at a painting and the detail drops away, leaving the big shapes. Each layer of summarization does the same thing, so a future me can grasp the note in seconds.

## Where it fits
- Works on resources inside the [[PARA Method]]
- Layer 4 is often where an [[Evergreen Notes|evergreen note]] is born
- In a [[Zettelkasten]], layer 5 happens naturally: the summary becomes a new atomic note that links back to its source

## Caution
Over-highlighting is the classic failure. If more than a fifth of a layer carries into the next, I'm not compressing — I'm colouring in.
