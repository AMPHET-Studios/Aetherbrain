# Environmental Storytelling

Telling story through the space itself: arrangement, wear, and clues the player pieces together. Almost nobody reads the lore dump; almost everybody notices tools left mid-task on a workbench.

## Three questions for every space
1. Who built this, and what for?
2. What happened here?
3. What is happening now?

## The ruins' three eras
- **Builders:** precise geometry, energy channels, inscriptions — the [[Ancient High-Tech Aesthetic]]
- **Fall:** scorch marks radiating from the portal, collapsed terraces, machines stopped mid-cycle
- **Reclamation:** roots through stone, birds nesting in conduits — grown with [[Unreal PCG Vegetation]]

## Techniques
- Vignettes: small staged scenes that imply an event
- Contrast between order and disorder draws the eye
- Light and [[Composition]] lead players toward clues
- Repetition teaches: once the player learns "cyan means power", every cyan seam becomes a hint

## Guardrails
- One clear read per vignette
- Never trade gameplay readability for story detail
- Story beats should reward curiosity, not gate progress

A core pillar of the [[Portal Game Vertical Slice]]. The moment-to-moment counterpart is [[Game Feel]]: story tells players *why* the gate matters; feel makes opening it satisfying.
